- What is the dew point?

> The dew point is the lowest temperature to which a mass of air can be cooled, at a given pressure and humidity, without liquid water forming due to saturation.
>
> The concept of dew point is a fundamental concept in the operation of refrigerated compressed-air dryers and in atmospheric condensation that creates hydrometeors. It is a key factor in a building’s thermal insulation, determining whether there is a risk that ambient humidity in a room will condense as it passes through the successive layers of an exterior wall, each of which has a specific temperature gradient that decreases toward the outside. If this occurs, the condensing moisture can cause deterioration of the layer in which the phenomenon occurs.
> (Source: Wikipedia)

- What about the freezing point?

> The freezing point, also known as the freezing-point temperature or the frost point, is a meteorological parameter calculated based on humidity, pressure, and temperature. The freezing point of air is the temperature at which, while keeping current barometric conditions unchanged, the air becomes saturated with water vapor relative to ice. The freezing point is therefore the equivalent of the dew point for the condensation of water vapor directly into ice crystals rather than microdroplets. It is the phenomenon of deposition, which occurs when the freezing point is reached, that creates hoarfrost.
>
> The frost point is therefore defined only below the freezing point. It coexists with the dew point at these temperatures, but since the saturated vapor pressure relative to ice is lower, the frost point is reached more quickly. Hygrometric capacity determines saturation phenomena: the colder it is, the less moisture the air can hold (absolute humidity), and the two converge at zero degrees Celsius.
> (Source: Wikipedia)
